Pre-lab Assignment for 9a:
1.
Calculate the molecular weights for the following compounds (a) Fe(NO3)3@9H2O (b)
K2C2O4@H2O (c) K3[Fe(C2O4)3]"3H2O (d) K3[Fe(C2O4)3] .
(a) 403.99
(b) 184.23
(c) 491.24
(d) 437.20
2.
Calculate the theoretical yield of K3[Fe(C2O4)3]"3H2O assuming the masses of reagents
are as written in the preparation.
# moles Fe(NO3)3@9H2O = (3.0 g)/403.99 = 7.4 mmol
theoretical yield = 7.4 x 10-3 x 491.24 = 3.6 g
3.
Dehydration in an oven does not always remove all water present. If 1.02 g
dehydrated sample was obtained from 1.14 g K3[Fe(C2O4)3]"3H2O, calculate how
many moles of sample went in to the oven, and how many moles of water were lost. 
Determine the ratio of moles of water lost per mole of sample. What is the formula of
the dehydrated sample?
1.14 g K3[Fe(C2O4)3]"3H2O represents 2.32 mmol.
(1.14 - 1.02) g = 0.12 g water lost
0.12 g / 18 = 6.67 moles water
therefore ratio of water: K3[Fe(C2O4)3] = 6.67 / 2.32 = 2.9 
Thus, we can assume that all the water has been lost.
4.
Draw a flow chart of the procedure in your notebook.

2.
Calculate how much potassium permanganate solution (assume a molarity of 0.0200 M
) must be delivered to add 80% of the titre for a 0.2500 g sample of K3[Fe(C2O4)3]
(0.5 marks).
2 MnO4
- + 16 H+ + 5 C2O4
2- 6 2 Mn2+ + 10 CO2 + 8 H2O
Therefore, 5 moles of oxalate are reacted with exactly 2 moles permanganate.
Since 3 moles of oxalate are furnished by each mole of K3[Fe(C2O4)3], 
then 5/3 moles of K3[Fe(C2O4)3] react with exactly 2 moles permanganate
now # mol K3[Fe(C2O4)3] to be titrated is 0.2500 g / 437.20 g.mol-1
thus # mol permanganate required will be # mol K3[Fe(C2O4)3] x 3/5 x 2 for
complete reaction.

= 0.2500 g / 437.20 g.mol-1 x 3/5 x 2
= 0.686 mmol
or for 80% reaction, 0.55 mmol, which translates to a volume using (# mol) /
(molarity [MnO4]-)
thus volume = 0.549 mmol / 0.0200 M = 27.4 mL
3.
Draw the structure of [Fe(C2O4)3]3- in sufficient detail to show which atoms are bonded
to each other and to show the geometry around the metal centre (0.5 marks).
Look for the oxalate drawn in detail and octahedral geometry.

3.
Explain the point of drawing a calibration curve (0.5 marks).
This allows one to average a set of data for a series of solutions of known
concentration, and then use it as a calibration device to compare those parameters with
that of the unknown. The object of the exercise being to determine the
concentration of the unknown.

Ascorbic acid is oxidised to the following structure:

The reduction of the iron(III) to iron(II)
(b) Why is it necessary to have an oxidisable substance (such as ascorbic acid) present in the
samples? (0.5 marks)
To make sure that all the iron is analysed, whether it is initially as iron(II) or (III).
