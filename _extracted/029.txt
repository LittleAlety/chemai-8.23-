 NOTE:   Always =
record=20
measurements to the correct precision and use glassware that will =
provide the=20
needed precision while still providing the quickest measurement.  =
The=20
precision or amount of digits past the decimal can tell you what type of =

glassware or instrumentation to use. The table on precision of glassware =
in the=20
introduction provides the precision of various types of glassware. In =
deciding=20
what glassware to use, use the required precision, capacity of =
glassware, and=20
time for measurement to make your decision. 
 For=20
example, 10 mL could be measured with a 250 mL graduated cylinder =
because it has=20
a precision of =B1 1 mL =
that=20
is  less than 10 mL volume to be measured but a 10 mL graduated =
cylinder=20
would be much better with a precision of =B1 0.05 mL. They would both take =
about the same=20
amount of time to use but the 10 mL would be much more precise.  =
Any=20
glassware that is more precise (it has a precision less than =
 =B1 1 mL) could also be used.  =
The only=20
glassware that can=92t be used to measure a 10 mL volume is a =
beaker.  Its=20
precision is =B1 10 mL, =
so=20
measuring a 10 mL volume would not be useful and so the actual volume =
could=20
range from ~5 to 15 mL.  Side Note: a measurement of about =
10 mL is=20
a really sloppy measurement.  A small beaker could be used for=20
this. 
 Usually=20
you want to obtain the best required precision in the least amount of=20
time.  For example, when measuring 10.0 mL of a liquid, a 10.00 mL=20
volumetric pipet takes a lot longer to deliver the same liquid when =
compared to=20
10.0 mL from a 10 mL graduated cylinder.  The pipet is also harder =
to=20
clean, so the graduated cylinder would be the best choice to =

 You will=20

 A=20

 What is recrystallization?   A process by which a minimum amount of hot =

such that the crystals formed are much more pure. The solution is cooled =
slowly=20
to room temperature in the lockers to prevent impurities from being =
trapped in=20
the crystal structure.  This process removes impurities and results =
in the=20
formation of larger crystals.
