Ferric Oxalate -=20

=93well=20

 Ferric oxalate is also known to form =

 The B+S powdered grade was made with =

 Ferric =
oxalate and=20
 oxalic acid and color of the solution Ferric =
oxalate will=20
 vary in color according to the amount of oxalic acid =
present. With=20
 little of no oxalic acid it will be a golden amber color. =
With=20
 moderate mounts of oxalic acid it will be a yellow, with =
large=20
 amounts it will turn fluorescent green. 
 The following are procedures for =
testing and=20

 Testing =
for free=20
 oxalic acid: Add 1 drop of a 10% to 20% silver =
nitrate to=20
 a 2 ml sample of the ferric oxalate solution. If a white =
precipitate=20

 oxalic acid present. One can verify this test by adding a =
drop of=20

nitrate=20

room=20
 temperature when the test is made. 
 Testing =
for ferrous=20
 oxalate Ferrous oxalate is the result of the decay =
by=20
 light or age of ferric oxalate. This causes the =
precipitation of the=20
 platinum or palladium metal that forms the image in a =
platinum=20
 print. Your ferric oxalate should be relatively free of =
ferrous for=20
 good printmaking. 
 There is a traditional test for the =
presence of=20
 ferrous oxalate. This involves testing with potassium =
ferricyanide.=20
 This test is unreliable in that it gives false positives =
when EDTA=20
 has been added. It also can produce a cyan color when the =
material=20
 is still acceptable for making prints. 
 A better test is to make a fog test =
by coating=20
 a piece of paper and developing without any exposure. If =
there is a=20
 gray tint to the coated part of the paper after clearing, =
the ferric=20
 oxalate has turned bad. 
 Removing =
free oxalic=20
 acid. You can add small quantities of ferric =
nitrate with=20
 stirring. Re-test after each addition of ferric nitrate. =
This method=20
 will leave a minute amount of nitric acid in the solution. =
It is=20
 harmless health wise but it is a oxidizer like chlorate, =
etc., that=20
 it could in sufficient quantities make the ferric oxalate=20
 contrastier. 
 Dissolving ferric=20
 oxalate There are several ways to do this: 1. =
Add water=20
 and stir. Now stir every few hours until it is dissolved. =
This may=20
 take as long as 12 to 24 hours. 2. Zap it in the micro =
wave.=20
 Loosen cap and give the bottle 10 seconds zaps with shaking =
between=20
 zaps. Zap it until it is hot to hold. 3. If you are lucky =
enough=20
 to have a laboratory hotplate stirrer, this will work dandy. =
Set the=20
 temp to about 140 Deg F. Older texts warn of using heat =
as it=20
 will cause the generation of ferrous oxalate. This may have =
due to=20
 the impurities in older versions of the product. We have =
found that=20
 it will take quite a bit of heat with decaying into =
ferrous.

 Ammonium=20
 oxalate To make: neutralize oxalic acid with =
strong=20
 ammonium or ammonium carbonate, filter and dry the=20
 crystals. Procedure for =
making=20
 ferric oxalate* This procedure requires a modest =
amount of=20
 chemical skills and prudent safety precautions should be=20
 observed. 
 Take 200 gm of ferric ammonium =
sulfate=20
 [FeCH4NO8S2] and dissolve in 300 ml water in a 500 ml =
beaker. 
 Add 100 ml of strong ammonium =
hydroxide [880=20
 Ammonia] and stir. (Caution the ammonia is strong and =
ghastly, do=20
 this in a well-ventilated area.) When the ammonia is added, =

 Keeping adding the ammonia until no =
new=20
 precipitate is formed. Too much ammonia won=92t =
hurt. This next=20
 step is to wash the gel. You will want to get out the excess =
ammonia=20

 First let the mud settle to the =
bottom of your=20
 beaker. This may take an hour or so. Carefully pour off the =
clear=20
 solution on top. 
 Now add more water to bring it to the =

On the=20
 last time do not add more water. 
 Now take a 1 foot square piece of an =
old tee=20

a=20

heavy=20
 rubber band.

 gloves on at this point! 
 Now pour and scoop out the washed mud =
from the=20
 beaker into the pocket. 
 Remove the rubber band and form a =

 fabric with the mud inside.

 the fabric is old as it can rip or burst.

 Pour @ 400 ml of water through the =

 pouch. The pouch will now be suspended in the water.

band as=20

 mud You don't need to measure the oxalic acid, you =

90% of=20
