The Experiment 
The purpose of the experiment is to determine the 
magnetic moment of a transition metal complex by a 
method of sufficient accuracy to establish the valence of 
the metal ion. The compound K3Fe(Cz0J3.3Hz0 was 
chosen for this purpose, since it is easily prepared in high 
purity (7), the described synthesis resulting in large well- 
formed single crystals. In addition it has the advan- 
tage of having a spin free d5 configuration which should 
give a large value for the susceptibility and make possi- 
ble accurate measurements of the magnetic moment 
with equipment consisting of an analytical balance of 
0.1 mg sensitivity and a magnet which has a peak mag- 
netic field of 3 KG or greater. The d5 configuration is 
also favorable for comparison of the value of the experi- 
mental magnetic moment with that calculated by eqn. 
(7), because if we have a d5 complex where all d-electron 
spins are unpaired (as in the case of K8Fe(C204)3.3Hz0) 
the total orbital angular momentum will be equal to 
zero; and, therefore, there should be no orbital contribu- 
tion to the magnetic moment. 
The student is asked to measure the magnetic suscep- 
tibility of KsFe(C204)3.3Hz0 by two modifications of 
the Gouy method. 
Since it was thought to be pedagogically desirable to 
make direct use of eqn. (3), an experiment was carried 
out where H and Ho were measured with a Gaussmeter. 
In addition it was felt necessary to familiarize the 
student with a relative method of obtaining X, so the 
student was asked to prepare a compound of known 
magnetic susceptibility, HgCo(CKS), (a), and use this 
compound as a calibrant. 
Finally, the student is asked to demonstrate for him- 
self the low intensity associated with a spin forbidden 
electronic transition by measuring the visible absorption 
spectrum of I<3Fe(CzOa)3.3H20. Since this compound 
bas all five 3d levels filled with one electron, each with all 
spins unpaired, transitions of an electron from one d 
level to another expected in the visible region (6) must 
go with spin flipping in order that the Pauli exclusion 
principle be obeyed in the excited state. Since a spin 
flip is a reasonably slow process compared to the dnrsi 
tion of an electronic transition, such transitions are very 
unlikely and are said to be spin forbidden.
