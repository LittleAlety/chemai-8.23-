Preparation of Group A complexes 
 
Bis-[N-benzylsalicylideneiminato]nickel(II) which is typical of all the complexes of 
Group A, is prepared as follows. 
6.11 g (0.05 mol) of salicylidene is treated with 5.36 g (0.05 mol) of benzylamine in 40 
mL of ethanol. The solid Schiff base forms immediately and is filtered using a Whatman 
#1 paper and recrystallised from ethanol. The Schiff base is then reacted with 7.3 g 
(0.025 mol) of nickel(II) nitrate in 80 mL water. The olive green precipitate of the 
coordination complex forms immediately. After cooling in an ice-bath, the solid is 
filtered using a Whatman #1 paper and recrystallised from ethanol. The yield of complex 
is 5.7 g. All other complexes in this group are prepared in a similar manner, using the 
appropriate aldehyde and amine and the correct molar ratio of ligand to metal ion. 
Analytical procedures to be followed for each Group of complexes 
Group A complexes: 
Collect the Infra-red spectrum, collect the UV-visible spectrum, determine the magnetic 
moment by the Gouy technique, determine the nitrogen content by the Kjeldahl method, 
the carbon content by the Leco carbon measurement, and the metal ion by FAAS. 
Group B complexes: 
Collect the Infra-red spectrum, collect the UV-visible spectrum, determine the magnetic 
moment by the Gouy technique, determine the conductance of a 0.001M solution. For 
complexes 7-10, determine the nitrogen content by the Kjeldahl method, the metal ion 
by FAAS, and the halide counter anion by conductance titration with standard silver 

nitrate. For complexes 11-15, determine the two metal ions by FAAS and the oxalate 
content by the Leco method. For complexes 16-18, determine the nitrogen content by the 
Kjeldahl method, the metal ion by FAAS, and the chloride counter anion by conductance 
titration with standard silver nitrate. 
The following are discussions and hints on the dissolution methods to be followed prior 
to the FAAS determinations, and the Kjeldahl and Leco experiments. 
Dissolution of complexes prior to FAAS analysis 
All the complexes are digested with concentrated nitric acid prior to FAAS analysis. 
Consider complex number 9 in Group B as an example. 0.2000 g of complex is digested 
with 15 mL concentrated nitric acid for 30 min and made up to 1 L in a volumetric flask 
with deionised water. 10 mL of this solution is then diluted to 100 mL in a volumetric 
flask with deionised water yielding a 4.5 mg/L-1 (ppm) solution ready for FAAS analysis.

Possible variations relevant to transition metal complexes 
1. If there are only NH3 ligands e.g. [Co(NH3)5Cl]Cl2, in the complex, simply introduce 
the sodium hydroxide solution and distil. 

2. Complexes containing C-N or C=N bonds, that is the Group A complexes and the 
ethylenediamine complexes of Group B, must be first broken down by digestion with 
with concentrated sulfuric acid in the presence of potassium sulfate (boiling point 
elevation) and selenium catalyst to form the ammonium hydrogensulfate salt, prior to 
undertaking the standard procedure.

Hints for Kjeldahl procedure 
Before commencing it is important that the quantity of sample required to give a suitable 
titration volume be calculated. It also may be more efficient to do two titrations from one 
digest rather than two individual digestion/titration proedures. For samples requiring 
digestion in sulfuric acid it is important to cool the solutions and dilute with a small 
amount of water before adding the NaOH. This can be done for an individual 
digest/titrate by adding water to the reaction-distillation flask and then adding the NaOH 
solution. For two titrations from the one digest, dilute accurately to 50 mL, then transfer 
a 20 mL aliquot to the distillation flask and finally add the NaOH solution. Aim for a 10 
to 15 mL final titration. It is also important to note that the larger the sample the more 
digestion time required and typically the sample should be < 0.5 g of compound. 
However, for complexes of Group A, where the %N is low, this is not feasible and you 

will need to use a larger reaction flask (e.g. 400 mL) for the digestion and heat it more 
aggressively. 
Leco gravimetric analysis 
The content of carbon is determined as carbon dioxide. The sample crucible is placed 
into a quartz combustion tube and heated by an induction furnace under a stream of 
purified oxygen. At the elevated temperatures the oxygen combines with the carbon in 
the sample to form carbon dioxide which is collected in an absorption bulb. 
Weigh into a Leco crucible the following items according to the carbon content of your 
compound. Place a Leco cover on top of the crucible before putting it in the analyzer. 
Standardisation 
Masses of material required for complexes of high carbon content (those of Group A) 
Accurately weighed 0.02-0.03 g glucose together with 0.8 g iron, 0.4 g copper and 0.4 g 
tin accelerators covered with silica. 
Masses of material required for complexes of low/medium carbon content (the oxalato 
and ethylenediamine complexes of Group B) 
Accurately weighed 0.08 g calcium carbonate together with 0.8 g iron, 0.4 g copper and 
0.4 g tin accelerators covered with silica.
